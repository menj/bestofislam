The challenge: Muhammad was alone in the cave. Nobody saw an angel. Muslims demand evidence for the resurrection of Jesus, so where is the evidence for Gabriel?

It is a fair question, and it applies to every revelation anyone has ever claimed.

Moses at the burning bush was alone. Isaiah's vision in the temple was his own. Paul on the road to Damascus saw a light his companions did not understand. No prophetic call in the Bible comes with an audience.

A standard that disqualifies Muhammad for having no witness in the cave disqualifies them all. Christians who apply it only to one man are not applying a standard.

Islam rests its case on the message. The Quran offers itself as the evidence, and challenges its hearers to produce anything like it.[1] The argument is about the text you can read, the man's character, and what followed. You can reject that argument. It is the one Islam makes.

The well-known hadith of Gabriel describes a stranger in white, with no sign of travel on him, who sat before the Prophet and asked about Islam, faith and excellence, in front of the companions. After he left, the Prophet told them it was Gabriel, come to teach them their religion.[2]

That is a report, and the critic will reject the chain. It still answers the question asked: according to the sources, people did see him.

The sources say Muhammad came down from Hira terrified, asked to be covered, and feared for himself.[3] His wife reassured him, and he was taken to a Christian relative to ask what had happened.

Someone inventing a revelation does not usually begin by doubting it and fearing for his sanity. The earliest account preserves a fear that later piety would have had every reason to delete.

Muslims ask Christians for evidence of the resurrection because Christianity stakes salvation on a historical event: if Christ has not been raised, Paul says, faith is futile.[4] The claim is historical, so historical evidence is the right demand.

Islam stakes itself on a message. The right demand there is to read the message and judge it. Matching the evidence to the claim is the only fair standard, and it applies to both faiths.

The bottom line

No one else saw the cave, as no one else saw the bush, the temple vision, or the road to Damascus. What Islam offers in place of a witness is a text, open to anyone, with a challenge attached.

Read it and answer the challenge. That is the evidence being offered.
