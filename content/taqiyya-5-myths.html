"Taqiyya" is cited online as proof that Muslims may lie to non-Muslims at will. The sources describe something much smaller. Here are the five claims most often made, and what the texts say.

The Quran's word is tuqāt, "protection": "Let not the believers take unbelievers as allies instead of believers, and whoever does that has nothing from God, unless you guard yourselves against them as a precaution."[1] The verse allows a believer under threat to protect himself.

The broader rule is in the verse on compulsion: "Whoever disbelieves in God after his belief, except one who is compelled while his heart is secure in faith."[2] It concerns life and death.

The verse above was revealed about Ammar ibn Yasir, who was tortured in Mecca and spoke words of denial to survive. When he came to the Prophet in distress, he was told: "If they return, then do the same."[3] His mother Sumayya, who refused, was killed and is counted the first martyr, and both are honoured.

The dispensation is real, and it is a dispensation for the tortured and does not apply to everyday life.

The Prophet said: "Truthfulness leads to righteousness and righteousness leads to Paradise. A man keeps on telling the truth until he is recorded as truthful. Lying leads to wickedness, and wickedness leads to the Fire."[4] The Quran says: "Why do you say what you do not do?"[5]

The hadith that allows a limited kind of untruth names three cases: in war, in reconciling people, and between spouses to preserve harmony.[6] The jurists read these narrowly, as speech that is not a direct falsehood, and they apply no further.

The Quran commands: "Fulfil the covenant of God when you have made it."[7] The Prophet said that a hypocrite breaks a promise.[8] He also kept the terms of the treaty of Hudaybiyya even when they seemed to go against the Muslims, and returned a man who came to him as a refugee.

A principle of taqiyya that allowed treaty-breaking would contradict both the Quran and his practice.

There is nothing hidden about it. The doctrine is stated openly in the standard commentaries, and the same commentaries limit it to compulsion. Critics who treat taqiyya as a secret plan are quoting a rule that sits in plain view in the books. It is also worth keeping the word apart from taqwā, which sounds similar and means something else: God-consciousness, the quality the Quran asks of every believer.
