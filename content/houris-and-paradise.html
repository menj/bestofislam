The "72 virgins" has become a joke and a slander. It is not a Quranic number, and it is not the main thing the Quran says about Paradise.

The Quran speaks of "ḥūr ʿīn", companions with wide, lustrous eyes (44:54; 52:20; 56:22). It calls them "restraining their glances" (55:56). It gives no number.

The figure of seventy-two comes from a single report in al-Tirmidhi that many scholars call weak or unusual in its chain.[1] A claim built on one doubtful report is not a doctrine.

The Quran does not restrict spouses in Paradise to men. "Enter Paradise, you and your spouses, rejoicing."[2] It promises "pure spouses" to the believers (2:25; 3:15; 4:57). Scholars have said that the women of the believers will be there and that God will give each person a spouse suited to them.

The Prophet reported that God said: "I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no heart has imagined."[3] The Quran's pictures, of gardens, rivers, silk and companions, are taken from what people know. Faruqi, writing on how the Quran speaks of the transcendent, noted that its descriptions of God and the unseen use earthly words while denying that they apply "simpliciter", the way a pointer works, and that the proper stance is bilā kayf, accepting them without asking how.[4]

The idea that a suicide attacker earns a number of virgins has no place in the sources. The Prophet said that a person who kills himself will be punished in Hell, and the Quran says "Do not kill yourselves."[5] The Quran also forbids killing the innocent, and the scholars of every school have rejected suicide attacks on civilians.[6]

A theory that ḥūr means "white grapes" in Syriac is rejected by scholars of Arabic and Syriac. The word is ordinary Arabic and appears in the poetry of the pre-Islamic period, and the theory does not fit the verses' sense.
