The Christian reply is a good one, and it deserves to be stated properly. Paul says the Law shows us what sin is, that we need it for that, and that it cannot save. Jesus said he came to fulfil the Law, and fulfilment changes its role without cancelling it. Jeremiah promised a new covenant.

That is the case. Here are 6 of Paul's own sentences to set beside it, and beside Jesus.

In Matthew, Jesus says he did not come to abolish the Law or the Prophets, that not the smallest letter or stroke will pass from the Law until everything is accomplished, and that whoever relaxes the least of its commandments will be least in the kingdom.[1]

That is the standard. The verse goes on past "fulfil", and the part after it is the part that matters.

Paul tells the Romans that believers have been released from the Law, having died to what held them.[2] Released is a strong word for a Law still in force.

To the Galatians he says the Law was a guardian until Christ came, and that now faith has come, believers are no longer under it.[3] A guardian you are no longer under has stopped governing you.

Ephesians says Christ abolished in his flesh the law of commandments and ordinances.[4] The Greek verb is the same family of word Jesus used when he said he did not come to abolish. Christians argue over whether Paul wrote Ephesians. Either way, it is in their canon.

Colossians tells believers not to let anyone judge them over food, drink, festivals, new moons or Sabbaths, calling these a shadow of what was to come.[5] The Sabbath is one of the Ten Commandments. Jesus kept it. Paul's churches were told it no longer bound them.

Galatians again: all who rely on works of the Law are under a curse.[6] Set that beside the man in Matthew who is least in the kingdom for relaxing a single commandment.

And Jeremiah says the Law will be written on hearts

The Christian appeal to Jeremiah 31 cuts the other way. The new covenant there is the Law placed within the people and written on their hearts.[7] In Jeremiah the Law is internalised and kept.

The bottom line

The careful Christian reading says Paul changed the Law's role while keeping its purpose. Read the verses in order and ask a plainer question: would the Jesus of Matthew 5 have told a congregation that the Sabbath no longer bound them?

Paul did. That is the tension Muslims point to, and it comes from inside the New Testament.
