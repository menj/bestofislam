The atheist position on origins comes in 3 flavours. The universe is a brute fact and needs no explanation. Or it caused itself. Or it is one of countless universes and we happen to be in one that works.

Each of those is a real position held by serious people. Each also has a cost that its defenders do not like to name. Here are 5 places the bill comes due.

The claim that the universe is eternal used to be respectable. Aristotle held it. So did most physicists until the middle of the 20th century.

Then the evidence went the other way. Expansion, background radiation, the abundance of light elements: all of it points to a beginning around 13.8 billion years ago. The Quran had said the heavens and the earth were once joined and were then split apart.[1] Whatever you make of that, the eternal-universe option is now the one carrying the burden.

Things that begin have causes. Denying that is a decision to stop asking.

Everything in the universe could have been otherwise. The electron could have had a different mass. The universe could have had 5 spatial dimensions, or 2.

Something that could have been otherwise needs a reason it is this way and not that. You can push the reason back a step, and back again, but an infinite chain of things that each need explaining does not add up to an explanation. It adds up to an infinitely long IOU.

At some point you need something that is not contingent. Something that could not have been otherwise. That is what the word God is doing in this argument, before it does anything else.

The strength of gravity. The ratio of the electromagnetic force to the strong force. The cosmological constant. Change any of them by a hair and you get no stars, no chemistry, no anything.

The standard reply is that we could not observe a universe that did not permit observers, so of course we see one that does. That is true and it explains nothing. If you are facing a firing squad of 100 marksmen and all 100 miss, "well, I could not observe my own death" is not an explanation for why you are still standing.

Give chance enough time and it produces anything, the argument goes.

Except it does not. Chance produces noise. It produces the kind of thing you would expect from shaking a box of parts. What it does not produce is laws: regularities that hold everywhere, at every scale, in equations you can write on a card. The universe is a structure, and structure is what minds make.

Fine, say there are countless universes with different constants, and we are in one of the rare ones that works.

Now explain the multiverse. What generates it? What sets the rules for how universes get their constants? Why is there a universe-generating mechanism at all, when there could have been nothing?

You have replaced one finely tuned thing with a finely tuned machine for making them. The bill did not go away. It got bigger, and it is still addressed to the same recipient.

The bottom line

None of these is a proof, and I am not pretending otherwise. Each is a place where the atheist account has to say "it just is" and hope you do not press.

Press. Ask what caused the beginning, what grounds the contingent, why the constants sit where they sit, why there is law instead of noise, and what generates the multiverse. Every answer that does not stop at a necessary, intelligent cause stops at nothing at all, and calls that an answer.
