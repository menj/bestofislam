Islam does not separate worship from conduct. The Quran puts the command to pray beside the command to feed the poor, and calls the person who does one without the other a denier of the faith.[1] The seven commands below are among the ones the Quran repeats most. Each is short and each is hard.

"O you who believe, be steadfast in justice, witnesses for God, even against yourselves, your parents and your relatives."[2] The verse asks for fairness when it costs something.

It goes further for enemies: "Do not let the hatred of a people lead you to injustice. Be just; that is closer to piety."[3] Justice is a duty of taqwā (God-consciousness) and has no exception for those one dislikes.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself."[4] It is the Islamic golden rule, and the wording makes it a measure of faith, not a courtesy.

Another report widens it: "The best of people is the one who is most useful to people."[5]

After the command to worship God alone, the Quran puts parents: "Be good to your parents. If one or both reach old age with you, do not say 'ugh' to them or rebuke them, but speak to them kindly."[6] Elsewhere it notes the mother's labour.[7]

Notice what is not said. It does not say "when they are deserving." The obligation is unconditional, and it remains even if the parents are not Muslim.[8]

"Fulfil the covenant, for the covenant will be asked about."[9] The Prophet listed the signs of a hypocrite: "When he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, and when he is trusted he betrays."[10] In the language of the law, an agreement is an amāna, a trust.

"Do not spy on one another or backbite one another. Would any of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother? You would hate it."[11] The comparison is deliberately unpleasant.

The Prophet defined backbiting as saying something about your brother that he would dislike, even if it is true. If it is false, he said, it is slander.[12]

"Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly, nor pass it to the rulers to consume a portion of the wealth of others sinfully, while you know."[13] The Prophet cursed the giver and the taker of a bribe.[14]

The principle is that office is a trust. A judge, an official or a witness holds power on behalf of others and may not sell it.

"Those who consume the wealth of orphans unjustly are only consuming fire in their bellies."[15] The Prophet was himself an orphan, and the Quran reminds him: "So as for the orphan, do not oppress him."[16]

He said that he and the one who cares for an orphan would be as close in Paradise as two fingers, and he held his index and middle fingers together.[17]
