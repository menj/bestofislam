Critics ask where the copies went. Uthman sent standard copies to the provinces around 650. If the Quran is divinely preserved, why cannot Muslims show them?

Start with what survives, because it is more than the question expects.

Two parchment leaves held at the University of Birmingham, carrying parts of chapters 18 to 20, were radiocarbon dated in 2015 at Oxford to between 568 and 645, with 95.4 percent probability.[1] The Prophet died in 632. Uthman's standard copies were made around 650.

The test dates the animal skin, and the ink could be somewhat later. Even so, these leaves place the Quran's text on parchment within a generation of the man who recited it, and possibly before Uthman's copies were made at all.

The great codex now kept in Tashkent, long held in Samarkand, is the manuscript Muslim tradition has attributed to Uthman for centuries. The tradition holds that it passed to Kufa, that Timur carried it to Samarkand as a treasure, and that it stayed there for some four hundred years until it was taken to Russia in 1868.[2] About a third of it survives.

Its dating is argued. Palaeographers generally place its script in the 8th century. Radiocarbon tests have returned different ranges: one on a folio held in Tashkent gave 595 to 855, another on the codex itself a later range.[3] What the tradition and the tests agree on is a very early Quran, copied in the generations after Uthman from the text he fixed.

The Codex Parisino-Petropolitanus, now divided between Paris, St Petersburg, the Vatican and London, is dated by François Déroche to the third quarter of the first Islamic century. Together its surviving leaves cover roughly 45 percent of the Quran.[4] That is close to half the text, in a single manuscript, from within decades of Uthman.

The Quran was recited aloud from the start, memorised in full by many companions, and recited daily in prayer. The written copies were a check on the memory. That chain of reciters has never broken: millions hold the entire text by heart today.

Ask where Uthman's own parchment is and you are asking for the backup. The primary system was the recitation, and it is still running.

Bukhari records it: Uthman had standard copies made, sent one to each province, and ordered the other written materials burned.[5] The same report gives the reason. Hudhaifa had seen soldiers from Syria and Iraq quarrelling over recitation, and urged Uthman to act before the community split over its Book.

Burning a superseded copy is the accepted way of retiring it respectfully in Islamic law. Burning a Quran in the street to insult Muslims is a different act with a different intent, and the comparison conflates them.

The bottom line

Leaves from the Prophet's generation in Birmingham. A codex tradition has named as Uthman's for centuries in Samarkand. Nearly half the Quran in a single first-century manuscript. And every one of them carries the consonantal text Muslims recite today.

Ask what the Quran said in 650. The manuscripts answer that, and they answer it the same way.
