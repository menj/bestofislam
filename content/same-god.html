The claim that Muslims worship a different god gets made from 2 directions. Some Christians say it to keep Islam at a distance. Some Muslims say it, oddly, for the same reason. Both are wrong, and the Quran is the reason.

Believers are told to say to the People of the Book: we believe in what was sent down to us and what was sent down to you, and our God and your God is one.[1]

That is an instruction on what to say, and the thing to say is that the God is the same.

Arabic Bibles use Allāh for God throughout. Arab Christians pray to Allah in church, and did so before Islam existed. The word is the ordinary Arabic for the one God, and the argument that it names a different being would have to explain why Christians used it first.

Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, Jesus. The Quran does not introduce a new cast. It claims the same one, and says the same God sent them all. You can dispute what the Quran says about them. You cannot say it is talking about somebody else's God when it is talking about Abraham's.

Christians say God is three persons in one essence. Muslims say God is one without division. That is a real disagreement and it matters.

It is a disagreement about what God is like. Jews and Christians have the same disagreement, and nobody says Jews worship a different god from Christians. Describing one being differently is what theological argument is. It does not multiply the beings.

Creator of everything. One, eternal, uncreated. Merciful and just. Sends messengers. Judges the dead. Rewards and punishes. Hears prayer.

Write down what the Hebrew Bible says about God and what the Quran says about God and put the lists side by side. The overlap is most of both lists. The residue is the dispute in reason 4, and it is a dispute about a shared subject.

If a different description means a different god, then Calvinists and Catholics worship different gods, and so do Orthodox and Baptists, and so did Augustine and Aquinas. Christianity has never accepted that logic for itself. Applying it only to Muslims marks a boundary and settles nothing in theology.

The bottom line

Same God, disputed nature. That is the Islamic position, and it is the position the Quran instructs Muslims to state out loud. Anyone who says otherwise, from either side, has a reason that is not in the text.
