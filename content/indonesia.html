Ask most people in the West to name a Muslim country and they will name one in the Middle East. The country with the most Muslims in the world lies far to the east, across the Indian Ocean, spread over thousands of islands.

Here are 6 facts about Indonesia that change how Islam looks from the outside.

Pew Research Center counts about 239 million Muslims in Indonesia, more than in any other nation on earth.[1] By the country's own civil registry, just over 87 percent of Indonesians are Muslim.[2]

For all its Muslim majority, Indonesia's constitution does not establish Islam as the religion of the state. It declares a belief in one Almighty God, and the government formally recognises six religions.[2]

Aceh, on the northern tip of Sumatra, is more than 98 percent Muslim. Bali, a few islands to the east, is barely 10 percent Muslim and keeps its Hindu majority. In Central Papua, Muslims are about 12 percent of the population.[2]

A single national figure hides a country in which a Muslim family and a Hindu family may be neighbours on one island and strangers on the next.

Istiqlal, the national mosque in Jakarta, takes its name from the Arabic word for independence. It opened in 1978 to commemorate the nation's freedom, and it is the largest mosque in Southeast Asia, its main dome carried on 12 great pillars.[3]

It stands directly across the street from Jakarta Cathedral. That placement expressed the founding president's vision of a nation in which many faiths would share one capital.[4]

Indonesia's two great Muslim organisations, Nahdlatul Ulama and Muhammadiyah, do not always begin the fast of Ramadan on the same day. In some years their members start a day apart, and the country simply accommodates both.[5]

Nahdlatul Ulama is the largest Muslim association in the country.[5] A disagreement conducted in the open, between organisations of that size, is a sign of a confident religious culture.

The Quran is recited in Arabic in every Indonesian mosque. The nation itself speaks Indonesian, alongside many regional languages, and its Muslims are Javanese, Sundanese, Malay, Minangkabau, Acehnese and many more.[2]

The faith arrived in the language of revelation and settled into the languages of the islands.

The bottom line

The largest Muslim nation on earth is a republic that names one God and recognises six religions. Its national mosque faces a cathedral across a Jakarta street.

Anyone who thinks of Islam as a Middle Eastern religion should start here.
