Popular Muslim writing is full of claims that the Quran anticipated modern science. Some are interesting. Some are weak, and repeating them damages the faith they are meant to support. This article grades seven, from strongest to weakest, and says why.

A word first about the Quran's own claim. The scholar Ismail al-Faruqi argued that the "miracle" the Quran offers is its language: the challenge to produce a chapter like it (2:23) is addressed to the intellect of those who know Arabic, and it is a different kind of sign from the parting of a sea, which overwhelms the mind.[1] On that view science is not the Quran's proof, and a weak scientific claim costs nothing the faith depends on.

"Do you not see that God sends down water from the sky and leads it through the earth as springs?"[2] "God is who sends the winds that raise clouds, and spreads them in the sky as He wills, and you see the rain come out of them."[3] The verses describe evaporation, clouds, rain and groundwater accurately. Grade: sound. But the Hebrew Bible says something similar (Ecclesiastes 1:7), so it does not set the Quran apart.

The Quran describes human creation from a drop (nuṭfa), then a clinging thing (ʿalaqa), then a chewed lump (muḍgha), then bones clothed in flesh.[4] Embryologists such as Keith Moore, in a later edition of his textbook, reported close parallels, and Maurice Bucaille drew attention to them.

Critics note that Galen and others had described stages, and that the terms can be read many ways. Grade: interesting, not decisive. The sequence is a good starting point for a conversation, not a proof.

"It is not for the sun to overtake the moon, nor does the night outstrip the day. Each swims in an orbit."[5] The verse says that celestial bodies move on their own courses, which fits modern astronomy. Grade: fair. The word falak (orbit, sphere) was also used in older cosmologies, so the verse does not teach a new model.

"An ant said, 'O ants, enter your dwellings.'"[6] Ants do communicate by chemical signals and touch, and the story fits that. Grade: modest. The claim that the Quran knew it is not needed, since people observed ants in the ancient world.

"Not even the weight of an atom (dharra) is hidden from your Lord."[7] The Arabic word means a small ant or a speck of dust. Reading it as the subatomic particle is a modern layer. Grade: weak. The verse's point, that nothing escapes God's knowledge, needs no physics.

The Quran says God can "put together the very tips of his fingers."[8] The word is banān (fingertips). The claim that this refers to fingerprints is repeated often. Grade: weak. The verse is about God's power to remake a person and does not mention uniqueness of prints.

"We sent down iron, in which is great strength."[9] Some write that this means iron came from space. Grade: weak. The same Arabic verb is used of livestock (39:6) and clothing (7:26), where it plainly means "provided".
