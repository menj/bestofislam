Secular humanists are, in my experience, decent people. That is not in question. What is in question is whether their account of why anyone should be decent holds together.

I will state each position as its defenders put it, then say where it gives.

The standard humanist move: morality is whatever promotes human flourishing. We can study what makes people flourish. Therefore morality has a scientific basis.

The gap is between is and ought, and it has been named since Hume. Science can tell you that cruelty reduces flourishing. It cannot tell you that you are obliged to care about flourishing. Someone who says "I would rather flourish at your expense" has not made a factual error. He has declined an invitation, and the humanist has nothing to compel him with except more invitations.

Fallback position: morality is what a society agrees on. It evolves, it is negotiated, it is ours.

Then slavery was moral in 1750, because the society agreed. Then the person who stood against it was wrong, by definition, until enough people joined him. Nobody actually believes this. Everyone thinks the abolitionist was right before the consensus moved, which means they think there is a standard the consensus was failing to meet. Name the standard, and you have left consensus behind.

The most sophisticated version: moral sentiment is a product of natural selection. Cooperation helped our ancestors survive, so we are wired to feel obligation.

Fine. So is the urge to dominate, deceive and hoard, which also helped our ancestors survive. Evolution gave us both sets of impulses and no way to rank them. The moment you say "follow the cooperative impulse and resist the dominating one," you are appealing to something evolution did not provide, a reason to prefer one inherited urge over another. Explaining where a feeling came from is not the same as showing it is binding.

Humanists are fond of the arc of history bending towards justice. It is a good line. It is also a confession.

Progress means getting closer to something. If morality is just what we happen to value now, there is no direction, only change. To call the abolition of slavery progress instead of fashion, you need a fixed point that 1750 was further from and 1850 was nearer to. The humanist uses the language of progress constantly and cannot say what the progress is towards.

The bottom line

Each of these is a place where the secular account borrows something it cannot pay for: obligation, a standard above consensus, a reason to rank one impulse over another, a destination for progress.

The Islamic claim is not that atheists cannot be good. Plainly they can. The claim is that goodness needs a ground, and that when you go looking for it, you find a lawgiver, or you find nothing and keep walking.

Test it on yourself. Next time you are certain something is wrong, ask what makes it wrong, and keep asking. See where you land.
