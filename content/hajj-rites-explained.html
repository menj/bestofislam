Ismail al-Faruqi's glossary of Islamic English defines the ḥajj as the fifth pillar of Islam, consisting of niyya (intention), iḥrām, ṭawāf, wuqūf (the standing), aḍḥiya (sacrifice) and rajm (stoning), acts performed at Mecca and nearby on the ninth and tenth days of Dhu al-Hijja.[1] That makes a convenient outline. Here is what each is, and why.

Every act of worship begins with niyya, the resolve in the heart to perform it for God. The Prophet said, "Actions are by intentions."[2] Hajj begins when the pilgrim forms the intention at the boundary and says Labbayka Allahumma labbayk (Here I am, O God, at Your service).

Men wear two unsewn white cloths and women their ordinary modest dress. The pilgrim avoids perfume, cutting hair and nails, hunting, sexual relations and arguing.[3] The equal dress removes marks of rank, so that kings and labourers stand alike.

Pilgrims circle the Kaaba seven times, counter-clockwise, with the House on the left. The Kaaba is built by Abraham and Ishmael.[4] Circling is not worship of the building. Umar, kissing the Black Stone, is reported to have said, "I know you are only a stone, which can neither harm nor benefit; had I not seen the Prophet kiss you, I would not."[5]

Pilgrims walk seven times between two small hills, as Hagar did in search of water for her infant son Ishmael, until the spring of Zamzam flowed.[6] The rite keeps a woman's anguish and trust at the centre of the pilgrimage.

On the ninth of Dhu al-Hijja the pilgrims stand on the plain of Arafat from noon to sunset, praying and asking forgiveness. The Prophet said, "Hajj is Arafat."[7] A pilgrim who misses the day has not completed the Hajj.

The plain is the place the Prophet gave his Farewell Sermon, which said that no Arab is superior to a non-Arab, nor a white man to a black man, except in God-consciousness.[8]

On the tenth, the festival of sacrifice, pilgrims throw pebbles at three pillars at Mina, recalling Abraham's rejection of Satan's temptation, and then sacrifice an animal in memory of Abraham's offering of his son, which God replaced with a ram.[9] The meat is given to the poor. After shaving or trimming the hair, the pilgrim leaves the state of ihram.
