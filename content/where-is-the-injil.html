It is the most common challenge in any comment section. The Quran says God gave Jesus the Injil. So produce it. Show one page. And if you cannot, explain why God let it vanish while billions went astray.

It sounds decisive. It rests on a picture of the Injil that the Quran never draws.

The Quran says God gave Jesus the Injil, containing guidance and light, confirming the Torah before it.[1] It never says he carried a bound volume, dictated one, or handed one to his disciples.

The word injīl is the Arabic form of the Greek euangelion, the good news. Asking for the physical Injil assumes the Quran means a codex. It speaks of a revelation Jesus received and preached.

The commenter wants one sentence from outside Islam saying Jesus had a message from God. Mark supplies it in the first chapter: Jesus came into Galilee proclaiming the gospel of God, and told his hearers to repent and believe in the gospel.[2]

That is Jesus preaching an euangelion before a single gospel had been written. Whatever Christians make of it, the idea of a gospel given to Jesus is in their own first gospel.

Jesus wrote nothing that survives. Christians accept that without concluding God failed to preserve his teaching. They hold that it reached people through those who heard him.

The Islamic claim is the same shape. The Injil was the message. Parts of it survive in the gospels, mixed with narrative about Jesus written by others decades later. A Christian who thinks the missing autograph of Mark is no threat to his faith cannot consistently treat a missing autograph of the Injil as a threat to mine.

Here is the real Islamic answer to "why did God not preserve it?" The Quran presents itself as confirming what came before and as a guardian over it.[3] The promise of preservation in the Quran is attached to the Quran.[4]

So the Muslim position is that each earlier message was entrusted to its community, and the final one was guarded directly. You can reject that. It is a coherent claim, and it answers the question as asked.

The commenter says billions will burn because the Injil was lost. The Quran says God does not punish a people until a messenger has come to them,[5] and it judges each soul by what reached it.

Accountability follows the message a person received. That is a harder rule than it sounds, and a fairer one than the question assumes.

The bottom line

The challenge needs the Injil to be a lost book, needs the Quran to have promised its preservation, and needs damnation to fall on people for a text they never saw. The Quran says none of the three.

What it does say is that Jesus preached a gospel from God. On that point, Mark agrees.
