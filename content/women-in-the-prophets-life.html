The early sources are full of women, and not as background. They advise, narrate, teach, fund and, in one case, decide the fate of the written Quran. Here are seven in the life of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), in roughly the order they enter it.

Aminah bint Wahb was widowed before Muhammad was born. She took him, about six years old, to Medina to visit her husband's relatives, and died on the way back at a place called al-Abwa, where she was buried.[1]

He lost his father before birth and his mother in childhood. The Quran addresses it later: "Did He not find you an orphan and give you shelter?"[2] Muslims count his own childhood as part of why the sources are so insistent on the care of orphans.

Khadija bint Khuwaylid was a respected businesswoman of Mecca who hired Muhammad to manage her caravan and later proposed to him. When he came home trembling from the cave of Hira after the first revelation, she said: "You keep ties of kin, you bear the burdens of the weak, you help the poor and you support those struck by calamity. God will never humiliate you."[3]

She was the first person to believe, and she spent her wealth on the cause. He took no other wife while she lived. She died in 619, and the year is remembered as the year of sorrow.

Fatima was the youngest and best known of his daughters. She married Ali, and their sons were Hasan and Husayn. The Prophet said, "Fatima is a part of me."[4] She died within months of her father.

Her place in Islamic memory is as much for character as for descent. The Prophet called her one of the leading women of Paradise.[5]

After the treaty of Hudaybiyya, the Prophet told the Muslims to sacrifice their animals and shave their heads, and nobody moved. They were upset at the terms of the treaty. He went to his wife Umm Salama, and she advised him to go out, say nothing, sacrifice his own animal and call his barber. He did, and the Muslims followed.[6]

Scholars cite the episode as a case where a woman's counsel resolved a crisis at the highest level.

Aisha bint Abu Bakr is credited with over two thousand hadith, and senior Companions came to her with questions on law. She corrected others when she thought they had remembered the Prophet wrongly. Her later student Urwa ibn al-Zubayr said he never saw anyone more learned in law, medicine or poetry than she was.[7]

Her life included a false accusation, answered by the Quran with ten verses clearing her name.[8] She also took part in the battle of the Camel in 656 and is reported to have regretted it. She has her own article here.

Hafsa bint Umar was a wife of the Prophet and a literate woman. After the Prophet's death, the sheets of the Quran that Abu Bakr had had collected were kept by Umar and then, after his death, by his daughter Hafsa.[9]

When Uthman wanted to produce standard copies, he asked her for the sheets, and returned them after they were copied. The care of the written Quran passed through a woman's hands.

Mariya was a Christian from Egypt who came to the Prophet in about 628 as a gift from the ruler of Egypt, and became a Muslim. She gave birth to his son Ibrahim, who died as an infant. At his death the Prophet wept and said: "The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves, and we say only what pleases our Lord."[10]
