Three words cover much of Muslim devotion outside the five daily prayers: dhikr, duʿāʾ, and the funeral rites. Ismail al-Faruqi noticed that English "prayer" fits only one of them, duʿāʾ, which means calling on God. The formal worship, ṣalāh, is another thing.[1] Here are the six practices.

Dhikr is the repeated remembrance of God, by phrases such as subḥān Allāh (glory to God), al-ḥamdu lillāh (praise to God) and Allāhu akbar. The Quran says: "Surely in the remembrance of God do hearts find rest."[2] The Prophet advised saying the three phrases thirty-three times after each prayer.[3]

Duʿāʾ is personal asking, in any language, at any time. "When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls on Me."[4] The Prophet called it "the essence of worship."[5]

There are no intermediaries and no fixed form. People ask for guidance, health, forgiveness and one another.

When a Muslim dies, the body is washed by members of the same sex (a spouse may wash a spouse), the water is clean, and the washing is done with respect, starting with the right side.[6] The Prophet's instructions on the washing of his daughter Zaynab are recorded.[7]

The body is wrapped in plain white cloth, three sheets for a man, and three or five for a woman according to the schools, with no coffin required. The Prophet was himself buried in white cloth.[8] The simplicity makes the rich and poor equal at the end.

The prayer, ṣalāt al-janāza, is said standing. It has four takbīr (saying Allāhu akbar) with no bowing or prostration, and includes praise, a prayer for the Prophet and a prayer for the deceased and the community. The Prophet said that whoever's funeral is prayed by forty people who associate nothing with God will have their intercession accepted for him.[9]

Burial is as quick as can be done, in the earth, the body lying on its right side facing the Kaaba. Visitors are told to condole for three days. A widow keeps a longer mourning of four months and ten days.[10] The Prophet wept at the death of his son Ibrahim and said, "The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves."
