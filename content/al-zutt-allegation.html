There is a narration recorded by Ahmad ibn Hanbal[2] where the Prophet recites the Quran to a group of beings, and the narrator compares their appearance to men of al-Zuṭṭ, a South Asian people the early Arabs knew of. The narration says these beings yarkabūn around him. The verb's primary sense is to mount or to ride.

Around 2022 an English reading appeared that turns this into sexual assault. It traces back to a Coptic priest, got into English through Raymond Ibrahim, and spread from there through the usual channels.

Here is why it fails.

The root r-k-b throws off a large family of senses in classical Arabic. Mounting an animal. Boarding a ship. Committing a deed. And crowding or pressing upon something, which is how a wave is said to ride another wave and how a crowd is said to ride a man it is pressing around.

Pick the one English gloss that produces the most lurid result, then read it back into the Arabic as though it were the only option. That is the whole method.

Run it on any classical text in any language and you will get comparable results. Try it on Shakespeare sometime.

The narrator describes them as tall, thin, sparsely fleshed, unclothed, and says he could not see any genitals on them.

That detail kills the sexual reading on its own terms. The reading needs anatomy the text says is not there.

The comparison to a known people is doing what comparisons do: giving a reader a visual handle on something unfamiliar.

The narration puts this inside a well-attested event. The Prophet recited the Quran to a gathering of jinn, and the Quran narrates the same thing at 46:29 to 32, where a company of jinn turn toward him, listen, and go back to their people as warners.

The narration says he was reciting to them the whole time. Isolating one verb and discarding the activity the text states is editing, whatever name it goes by.

Polemic against the Prophet is about as old as the Prophet. Some of it was written by people with excellent Arabic, considerable learning, and every motive to find the worst available reading of any narration in the corpus.

None of them found this one.

It shows up in the 21st century, in English, from writers who do not work from the Arabic. A reading that occurred to nobody with the language for 14 centuries is a modern invention, and the burden sits with whoever claims otherwise.

Hadith get authenticated before they get interpreted. What is the isnād? What are the variant wordings across collections? How did the scholars grade it?

Polemical treatments of this narration have skipped that step entirely.

That is the reliable signal. When someone will not do the first step of the method, the goal was never the inquiry.

The bottom line

There is also a prior question the allegation never asks: whether the narration is sound at all. In a report Muslim records, Ibn Masud himself says that none of the companions was with the Prophet on the night of the jinn.[3] Editors of the Musnad grade narrations in this cluster as weak, noting that a sound chain establishes his absence.[4] The allegation builds a scandal on a report whose own tradition questions it.

A mistranslated verb. An anatomy the text denies. A context the text supplies and the polemic deletes. An interpretive history starting in 2022.
