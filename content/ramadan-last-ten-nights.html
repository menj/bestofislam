The last ten nights are the part of the month many Muslims wait for. The fast is in its final stretch, the mosque is full at night, and one of the ten nights is better than a thousand months. Here is what is known about it. Ismail al-Faruqi asked English writers to call the lunar months by their Arabic names, since the months follow the moon and the names are proper names. That is the practice followed here.[1]

"We sent it down on the Night of Power (Laylat al-Qadr). And what will make you know what the Night of Power is? The Night of Power is better than a thousand months."[2] The Quran began to be revealed in this month, and on this night, according to the commentators.[3]

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Seek the Night of Power in the last ten nights of Ramadan, in the odd nights."[4] The twenty-seventh is the night many choose. The exact night is not stated, and the scholars say it is hidden so that people strive on all of them.

Aisha said that when the last ten nights began, the Prophet "tightened his waist-wrapper, kept awake at night and woke his family."[5] He increased his worship and his giving.

Aisha asked what she should say if she found it. He told her: "Allāhumma innaka ʿafuwwun tuḥibbu al-ʿafwa, fa-ʿfu ʿannī": O God, You are pardoning, You love to pardon, so pardon me.[6]

The prayer is short, which is the point. It is a request for forgiveness, since a person who has been forgiven has the best of the night.

Iʿtikāf is staying in the mosque for the last ten days, leaving only for need. Aisha said that the Prophet kept it until God took him, and his wives kept it afterward.[7] A person does it for devotion: prayer, Quran and remembrance. A shorter retreat, even a night, is also valid.

It is a sunnah, a recommended practice, and not an obligation. Those who cannot do it for health or work can still give the nights as much worship as they can.
