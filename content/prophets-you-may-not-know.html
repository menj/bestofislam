The Quran names twenty-five prophets and says there were others.[1] Everyone knows Moses and Jesus. The five here appear briefly, and the brevity is part of the story: "There is no community to which a warner has not come."[2] The scholar Ismail al-Faruqi drew from verses like this the conclusion that no people was left without knowledge of the one God.[3]

"Mention in the Book Idris. He was a man of truth, a prophet, and We raised him to a high station."[4] That is nearly everything the Quran says. He is also counted with the patient.[5]

Many commentators identified him with Enoch, of whom Genesis says that "God took him."[6] Others disagree. The Quran gives neither his people nor his message.

Hud was sent to 'Ad, "their brother", and told them to worship God alone and to be grateful for what they had.[7] They asked for proof, and he answered that he relied on God.[8] The people were destroyed by a roaring wind, and Hud and the believers were saved.

Surah 11 is named for him. The pattern of the story is the same for all of these prophets: a warning, a refusal and consequences.

"Remember Ishmael, Elisha and Dhul-Kifl, all of them among the best."[9] The name means "the one of the portion" or "the guarantor". Commentators have identified him with Ezekiel, with Joshua and others.

The honest statement is that the Quran names him and says he was of the patient, and nothing more is known.[10]

Elisha (al-Yasaʿ) is named twice, among the prophets whom God preferred above the worlds.[11] He is the successor to Elijah in the Bible. The Quran adds nothing to the story.

His place is as a name in a line of prophets, and a reminder that the chain was long.

Elijah (Ilyās) is among the prophets who were upright, and the Quran gives a short passage: "He said to his people, 'Will you not be mindful of God? Will you call on Baal and leave the best of creators?'"[12] Baal was the god of the Canaanite worshippers whom Elijah confronted in the Bible.

The account is brief and recognisable, and it ends with a blessing: "Peace be upon Elijah."[13]
