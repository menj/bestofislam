Child marriage is a real problem in parts of the world, Muslim and not Muslim, and it is often defended with religion. Here is what the Islamic sources actually say, kept separate from custom.

The Quran does not state a minimum age. It speaks of orphans who "reach the age of marriage" and tells guardians to test their sound judgement before handing over their property.[1] Marriage in the Quran's framework is a mīthāq ghalīẓ, a solemn covenant.[2] A covenant needs a party who can understand and agree to it.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that a previously married woman may not be married until she is consulted, and a virgin until her permission is asked.[3] When a woman told him that her father had married her to someone she disliked, he gave her the choice to refuse.[4]

Some jurists allowed a father to contract the marriage of a minor daughter, with consummation delayed until she is able. Others, among them early scholars such as Ibn Shubruma and Uthman al-Batti, held that a marriage before maturity is not valid.[5] The point is that the question was argued inside the tradition and not settled.

The best-known report concerns the Prophet's marriage to Aisha, which is discussed with the evidence in its own article on this site. Scholars distinguish between what the Prophet did as a particular case, in a society with different norms, and what he commanded as law. The commands on consent and maturity are the law.

Egypt, Turkey and Tunisia set 18 as the minimum age, and Morocco did so with a court exception. Muslim jurists have defended such laws as in keeping with the purposes of sharia, which include protecting a person's welfare. Where child marriage persists, the causes are poverty, weak enforcement and custom, and it exists in non-Muslim societies as well.
