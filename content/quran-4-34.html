Quran 4:34 is the verse most often quoted against Islam on the treatment of women. It deserves a careful reading, because the usual translation hides the structure of the verse and the history of its interpretation.

It says men are qawwāmūn, those who stand for and support women, because of what they spend from their wealth. Of women from whom ill will is feared, it gives three steps: advise them, leave them in bed, and iḍribūhunna. The word is usually translated "strike them." The next verse (4:35) calls for arbiters from both families if a rift remains.[1]

Classical commentators held that the steps must be taken in order and that the last may not be used if the earlier ones work. Ibn Abbas said that if it is done, it is with a tooth-stick, a symbolic touch that leaves no mark.[2] The Prophet, in the Farewell Sermon, said that it should be "not severe."[3]

Aisha said that the Prophet never struck a woman or a servant with his hand.[4] He said of men who beat their wives: "Those are not the best of you."[5] He also said: "The best of you is the best to his wives, and I am the best of you to my wives."[6]

The Arabic root ḍ-r-b has many meanings, including to strike, to travel, to give an example and to separate. Some modern scholars read 4:34 as "separate from them," which fits the sequence of leaving the bed. Others keep the classical reading but say its use today is forbidden because it causes harm. The Quran uses the same root in 24:31 for a woman's veil falling on her chest, and in 14:24 for giving a parable, which is the reason the question is raised.

Many contemporary scholars hold that domestic violence is a crime and that the verse's steps are to be read in the light of the Prophet's practice and the prohibition of harm: "No harm and no reciprocating harm."[7] Muslim women's courts and counsellors in many countries treat a husband's violence as grounds for divorce.
